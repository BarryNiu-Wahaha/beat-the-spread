Group Project Proposal: Enterprise Data and Analytics Platform
Project Goal
Select a
large organization
and design an end-to-end data and analytics platform for that organization.
Your project should represent the types of data the organization would need for:
daily operations;
products/services;
decision-making; and
a new business application or analytical capability.
Your architecture should include
RDBMS, NoSQL, big-data processing, a data warehouse, analytics, and RAG/Generative AI
. Each technology should have a clear purpose based on the data and business need.
The final system will be developed and tested locally, but must also be
deployed on AWS as a working production application
.
Initial Proposal.
Submit a maximum two-page tentative proposal containing:
1. Organization and Business Opportunity
Select a large organization.
Briefly describe its major data needs.
Identify a
new business application, analytical capability, or decision problem
your project will support.
Explain why this capability would be valuable to the organization.
Your organization should generally remain the same after the proposal, although your data and architecture may evolve.
2. Data Sources
Identify the major datasets you expect to use.
For each dataset, briefly describe:
source/link;
type of data;
approximate size;
structured, semi-structured, or unstructured format; and
how the organization would use it.
Use public or institutionally available datasets such as Kaggle, data.gov, company/open-data sources, APIs, web-scraped data, or Emory data resources.
(e.g.,Citi Bikers rental subscription data: https://ride.citibikenyc.com/system-data). You are welcome to look for datasets on kaggle.com, data.gov, or scrape online data.
You can also explore some of the paid datasets available to Emory students:
GBL Resources (Big Data) Fall 2023.docx
Your project should include
at least one dataset with more than 1 million records
.
You may generate some synthetic data when suitable real data is unavailable, but the project should include substantial real-world data. You can lose points for overusing synthetic data.
3. Tentative Data Architecture
Provide a simple architecture diagram showing how you expect to use:
MySQL / RDBMS
for appropriate operational data;
one or more
NoSQL databases
for appropriate semi-structured, key-value, document, or large-scale data;
Hadoop/HDFS and/or Spark
, where appropriate;
an
ETL/ELT pipeline
;
a
Star or Snowflake data warehouse
;
a
vector database
such as ChromaDB or Qdrant;
Streamlit
for the user interface and analytics; and
AWS
for production deployment.
You are not expected to know all implementation details yet. Several of these technologies will be taught later in the course.
4. Analytics and RAG
Identify:
several business questions or metrics your dashboard should answer;
the type of visualizations/insights you expect to provide;
the unstructured data you expect to embed; and
examples of questions users should be able to ask through your RAG system.
5. Team Plan
Briefly describe how your team expects to organize the work.
Although tasks may be divided,
every team member is responsible for understanding the complete architecture and all major components of the project
.
Final Project Expectations
The final project will include:
operational data stored in both
RDBMS and NoSQL databases
;
a scalable data-processing component using technologies such as
Hadoop/HDFS and Spark
;
an
ETL/ELT pipeline
feeding a Star/Snowflake data warehouse;
business analytics and visualizations in
Streamlit
;
a
RAG capability
using embeddings and a vector database;
a working
AWS production deployment
;
project report;
video demonstration;
production URL;
source code; and
class presentation.
Each team member may be asked individually to
explain, demonstrate, or modify any part of the project
. Each team member will receive individual points based on their contributions and understanding of the project.
Note:
This proposal is tentative. You may refine the data, technology choices, and scope as you learn additional tools during the semester and after discussing changes with the instructor.
Still, I recommend keeping the project idea consistent so you can keep progressing.
